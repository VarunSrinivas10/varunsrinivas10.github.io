About me

Materials science researcher exploring how grain boundaries govern microstructural evolution and what that means for the design and performance of polycrystalline materials.

I am a PhD candidate in Materials Science and Engineering at the University of Michigan, working in the Shahani Lab under Prof. Ashwin Shahani. My dissertation focuses on grain boundary characterization and kinetics in three dimensions, combining synchrotron diffraction contrast tomography (DCT) with graph-based machine learning to understand how grain boundaries select, persist, and disappear during recrystallization and grain growth.

I develop graph neural network (GNN) pipelines that treat polycrystalline microstructures as edge-attributed graphs, enabling automated classification of grain boundary outcomes. This work connects crystallographic descriptors — misorientation, CSL character, curvature, and topology — to boundary-scale behavior in a data-driven framework.

Beyond grain boundaries, my interests include 3D tomographic reconstruction, feature engineering for microstructural descriptors, and MATLAB- and Python-based visualization pipelines for volumetric materials data. I collaborate closely with Yubin Zhang and colleagues at the synchrotron facilities that make 4D experiments possible.

More about my work can be found in the publications and projects sections. For inquiries or potential collaborations, feel free to get in touch.
